# Banking Trade Associations Petition SEC to Rescind Item 1.05

## Summary

On May 22, 2025, a coalition of five banking trade associations — the American Bankers Association, Bank Policy Institute, Independent Community Bankers of America, SIFMA, and the Institute of International Bankers — jointly petitioned the SEC to rescind the requirement to disclose material cybersecurity incidents within four business days under Item 1.05 of Form 8-K.

The petition was filed against the backdrop of the second Trump administration's SEC enforcement priorities, where commentators expect a different posture toward cyber disclosure than the prior administration.

## Why It Matters

The 4-day rule has been the load-bearing element of the cyber-disclosure regime since December 2023. A successful petition would not eliminate disclosure obligations, but it would meaningfully change the urgency and the materiality framework that drives client demand for continuous controls.

## Implications for PwC

- The continuous controls assurance methodology is durable regardless — the audit-readiness value remains.
- Pitch language should not lean on Item 1.05 as if it is permanent. Frame value as board confidence and audit posture, not as rule-specific compliance.
- Brief Marshall before scope confirmation on any continuous-controls-assurance pursuit that hedges on Item 1.05 surviving.
